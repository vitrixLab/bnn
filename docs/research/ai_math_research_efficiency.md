## Executive summary

AI efficiency research includes quantization, pruning/sparsity, low-rank factorization, knowledge distillation, and architecture design. These techniques can reduce parameter storage, arithmetic, or measured inference cost, but their benefits depend on the model, dataset, hardware, implementation, training recipe, and metric. A smaller parameter count does not necessarily imply lower latency or energy use; unstructured sparsity, for example, may not accelerate inference on hardware without suitable sparse kernels.

The evidence in the retained studies is a mix of **empirical findings**, **heuristics**, and **formal mathematical results**. This document does not infer a theorem from a reported experiment. Claims such as “without loss of accuracy” are limited to the specific model, dataset, metric, and evaluation protocol reported by the cited authors; they are not universal guarantees. Cross-paper ratios and accuracy numbers are not directly comparable unless their datasets, model variants, precision, hardware, batch sizes, latency measurement methods, and baselines align.

No general “strong consensus” that most methods yield Pareto improvements is asserted here. A Pareto improvement must be demonstrated against a stated baseline on comparable metrics. Claims about double descent, compression improving generalization, and universal quantization-error limits remain dependent on assumptions and problem setting; they should not be presented as settled universal results.

### How to read evidence labels

- **Formal result:** a theorem, proposition, or bound with stated assumptions. The assumptions and exact result must be checked in the paper.
- **Empirical finding:** measured results on specified datasets, models, hardware, or experimental protocols.
- **Heuristic / method:** an algorithmic procedure without a general guarantee of the claimed outcome.
- **Qualitative claim:** interpretation or motivation that is not itself a theorem or a directly comparable benchmark.
- **Comparison caveat:** reported values are retained as paper-specific observations, not a normalized leaderboard.

## Metric and comparison rules

1. **Accuracy:** specify dataset, split, metric (e.g. top-1 vs top-5), and baseline model/version.
2. **Parameters / storage:** distinguish parameter count from serialized file size; quantization and entropy coding affect storage differently.
3. **FLOPs / operations:** state counting convention and precision. Operation-count reductions are not automatically wall-clock speedups.
4. **Latency:** report device, runtime/library, batch size, input shape, warm-up, and measurement method. Do not compare CPU latency to GPU latency as if normalized.
5. **Energy:** state hardware, power measurement boundary, workload, and throughput. Energy claims cannot be inferred from parameter count alone.
6. **Compression / accuracy retention:** qualify “without loss” as a result under the cited paper’s stated experiment; do not convert it into a universal guarantee.
7. **Cross-paper comparisons:** mark as **reported under different settings** unless protocols and baselines are directly comparable. No normalized cross-paper ranking is claimed in this document.
